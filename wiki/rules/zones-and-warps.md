# Zones and warps

The world is split into zones: towns, fields and dungeons, each loaded on its own. You walk
from one zone to the next through warp gates, and a loading screen shows while the new
zone loads. Every zone has its own page under [[zones|Zones]].

## Positions and maps

Positions on the wiki are in **metres**, as the server keeps them (the server counts in
centimetres; divide by 100). x grows to the east and y to the north.

A zone is built from square map blocks of **160 m**. The world is a grid of 64 by 64
blocks, and each zone uses the blocks that have map files.

Each zone page draws the zone's minimap picture, which has **2.5 m per pixel** (64 pixels
per block). LIST_ZONE.STB gives the block the picture starts at (start x, start y). The
wiki places the picture with its top-left corner at:

```math
\text{left} = (\text{start}_x - 1) \times 160 \qquad \text{top} = (66 - \text{start}_y) \times 160
```

and the right and bottom edges are the width and height in pixels times 2.5 m.

**Worked example.** [[zones/2-city-of-junon-polis|City of Junon Polis]] has start block
(30, 30) and a 576 by 512 pixel minimap. Left $= 29 \times 160 = 4640$ m, top
$= 36 \times 160 = 5760$ m, right $= 4640 + 576 \times 2.5 = 6080$ m, bottom
$= 5760 - 512 \times 2.5 = 4480$ m.

> Open question: godot/scripts/ui/hud/minimap.gd places the minimap picture with start_x × 160 and 65 − start_y, while the wiki maps line up with the warp gates only when shifted one block, (start_x − 1) × 160 and 66 − start_y. The in-game minimap (and the M zone map, which uses the same placement) may be off by one block (160 m). Not verified in the game.

## Zone edges

The server refuses a move to a point outside the zone's rectangle: from the corner of the
lowest map block to the far edge of the highest, rounded down to whole sectors (the sector
size is in LIST_ZONE.STB). See "What the server checks" below.

## Warp gates

- A warp gate is a box in the zone's map files. You warp by walking into it: the box counts
  **3 m** bigger on every side, because many gates have a wall right in front of them.
- After you arrive you must first step out of every gate before one can take you again,
  and the client waits at least **5 seconds** between two warps.
- WARP.STB says which zone each gate leads to and the named spot there; you arrive on that
  spot.
- You can't use a gate while dead.
- Warping (by gate, scroll, quest or getting up at a save point in another zone) closes your
  [[rules/personal-shops|shop]], takes you off a [[rules/carts|cart]], stops attacks and
  skills being cast. A cart's passenger comes along and lands 1 m to the side.

Each zone page lists its warp gates and where they lead.

Other ways to change zones: warp scrolls and skills that move you (see [[skills|Skills]]),
and quest rewards that move you (see [[rules/quests|Quests]]).

## Loading screen

When the server has moved your character to another zone (and when you sign in), the game
shows the loading picture with the zone's name while it loads the zone. It goes away when
your character stands in the new zone, or after **10 seconds** at most.

## Save points and getting up after death

- Every zone has a **start point** and one or more **revive points** (named in LIST_ZONE.STB).
- Some NPCs (and some quests) set your **save point**: the revive point of that zone nearest
  to you. You see "Save point set: *zone*".
- When you get up after dying you choose: at the revive point of your current zone nearest
  to where you fell, or at your save point. The save point only works when it is on the same
  planet (Junon, Luna or Eldeon); otherwise you get up in the current zone. You land up to
  **5 m** from the point in each direction.
- Logging out while dead gets you up at the zone's revive point first.

Details are on [[rules/death|Death]].

## Signing in

You come back in the zone and at the spot where you logged out, with the HP and MP you had.
A new character starts on [[zones/20-birth-island|Birth Island]] (see
[[rules/accounts|Accounts]]).

## Day and night

Each zone has its own day. Time moves in world ticks of **10 seconds**. LIST_ZONE.STB gives
the length of a zone's day in ticks and the tick at which morning, day, evening and night
start. Night lasts from its start until morning of the next day. Morning and evening blend
the light from the time before into the time after.

```math
\text{day length (seconds)} = \text{ticks per day} \times 10
```

**Worked example.** [[zones/20-birth-island|Birth Island]] has a 160-tick day: 1,600 s, or
26 minutes 40 seconds. Morning starts at tick 0, day at tick 16 (2 min 40 s in), evening at
tick 133 (22 min 10 s in) and night at tick 157 (26 min 10 s in), so night lasts 30 seconds
before morning comes again.

The PvP arenas are always day and the Goblin Cave floors always night. The table of all
zones is in the data block.

The server's world time (used by monster and NPC behaviour that depends on the time of
day) counts ticks of 10 seconds of real time, the same for everyone.

> Open question: the game client starts its own clock at "day" when it starts and does not follow the server's world time, so the light you see and the time monsters and NPCs act on can differ, and two players can see different times of day.

## What the server checks when you move

- You only ask to walk to a point. The server moves your character there in a straight line
  at your own run speed, so you can't go faster or jump to another place.
- A point outside the zone's edges is refused.
- The server has no walls or ground, so walls and cliffs are the game client's job. When
  your character walks into a wall, the client tells the server where it stopped. The server
  only accepts a stop on your current path, never ahead of where it has you.

> Open question: the server does not check that you are near a warp gate when you use it, so a changed client could use any gate from anywhere (the server has no zone geometry to check the gate's position).
